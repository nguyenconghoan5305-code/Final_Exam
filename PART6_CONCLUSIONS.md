## Q1. Storage, laptop processing, and a cluster decision

**Answer.** Store the verified quarterly Financials as typed, `REPDTE=YYYYMMDD` partitioned ZSTD-3 Parquet, with separate complete Failures and Institutions tables, source provenance, manifests and checksums. Use Polars lazy scans for the measured observed-panel transformations on this laptop; keep pandas as a comparable baseline and local PySpark as a parity and execution-plan demonstration. For the tested workload, the evidence does not justify moving to a Spark cluster.

The accepted 170-quarter acquisition has 1,682,616 Financials records, while the fixed 26-field logical storage experiment compares three exactly equal representations. ZSTD-3 used **153,574,569 bytes**, 32.730% of CSV's **469,211,854 bytes**; Snappy used **203,331,205 bytes**, 43.335% of CSV. Full-read medians were **8.733188 s CSV**, **1.685006 s Snappy**, and **1.992929 s ZSTD-3**. The selective 2010Q1–2019Q4, eight-column, 262,595-row workload had medians **0.431895 / 0.084119 / 0.101553 s** in the same order. Five measured fresh-process reads per format/workload followed one untimed warm-up; parity and conversion were outside the timer. The selective comparison includes CSV's lack of native column projection. OS caches were not controlled, so these measurements are not cold-cache results and do not isolate compression from projection.

For the separate **six-column observed-panel workload**, five end-to-end fresh-process runs per engine on identical inputs yielded median wall times of **35.599 s pandas**, **2.947 s Polars**, and **62.499 s local PySpark**. Their median sampled process-tree RSS was **1,097.199 / 1,023.773 / 2,360.633 MiB**, respectively. The pandas-to-Polars time ratio was **12.08×** using median pandas wall time divided by median Polars wall time. Every saved output relation passed exact parity. The captured Polars plan shows projected Parquet scans, sorting, bank windows and quarter aggregation; executed Spark plans show scans, exchanges, sorts, windows and hash aggregation. The benchmark did not isolate the time attributable to any operator. Its timer includes startup, full materialization and three Parquet writes; the Spark path also includes driver collection and session shutdown.

The largest sampled process-tree RSS in this three-engine run was **2,603,708,416 bytes**, 15.5% of the recorded **16,780,767,232-byte** physical RAM. No measured run hit a memory or disk limit. A cluster would warrant evaluation if a specified larger or concurrent workload exceeds a measured local resource or time target after local optimization. A genuine scale-up experiment would compare identical outputs and full costs, including shuffle/network and operational overhead. No cluster runtime, speedup, cost, crossover size, or break-even point was measured here.

## Q2. Raw quality, exits, and look-ahead control

**Answer.** The reproducible audit finds substantial historical missingness, absent versus explicitly null fields, incomplete linkage to a later institution snapshot, and assistance events that must not be called failures. The pipeline preserves source rows and provenance, flags problems without silently changing values, builds one observed row per `(CERT, REPDTE)`, and keeps retrospective cohort evidence, as-of report selection, predictors and future outcomes in separate roles.

Among **1,682,616 Financials records**, `P3ASSET` is explicitly null in **971,925**, with **68 entirely null quarters**. Among **27,835 Institutions snapshot records**, `CHANGEC1` is absent from **3,482** source responses rather than explicitly null. Among **4,117 Failures/Assistance source records**, **488** have null `CERT`, but each retains a unique source `ID`; **637** have null `COST`. A present null is never recoded as zero. The accepted flag-only treatment retains **all 1,682,616 / 4,117 / 27,835 rows** with unchanged raw values, while recording overlapping issues by distinct source key. It retains historical banks missing from the later Institutions snapshot. These are measured quality and linkage limitations, not a claim that every flagged record is erroneous.

The P2.2 observed panel has exactly **1,682,616** unique bank-quarter rows; it records **33 resumed reporting gaps across 32 banks** rather than manufacturing missing zero quarters. The repaired P2.3 event evidence uses official FDIC transaction type to distinguish failure from assistance and acquisition-based failure resolution from ordinary merger. Its last-report assessment confirms **2,534** failure banks, down from the **superseded 2,775** preliminary count; **241** former confirmations become ambiguous. Those are bank-level last-report assessments, not the later count of model-positive source events. Unlinked events and uncertain exits remain explicit.

For prediction quarter-end `q`, `REPDTE` is the report's accounting date, **assumed availability** is `REPDTE + 120 calendar days` unless verified later revision timing applies, and `FAILDATE` is an outcome date. The as-of rule admits only a version available on or before `q`, including an exact-day boundary; it rejects or flags ambiguous eligible revisions. The official outcome window remains `(q, q + 4 calendar quarters]`. No later failure, exit assessment, Institutions snapshot status, or future-derived quality flag is a predictor. Synthetic and bounded real perturbations support the rule, but the fixed lag cannot reconstruct unpublished historical release dates or revisions.

The accepted activity policy retrospectively assesses **24,064 Financials-observed CERT identities** over **166** prediction quarters. Of **3,994,624** decisions, **1,660,450** are activity-eligible and **35,222** uncertain; **54,585** eligible decisions lack available feature history. **139** observed identities lack a fully supported activity interval, while supported identities can still have time-local conflicts. The cohort relies on current-vintage structural evidence and may change after historical corrections. It must not be described as the exact bank roster a historical supervisor could have known at every `q`. The model table further filters to resolved labels and report history; cohort selection and missing-vintage risks remain in interpretation.

## Q3. Honest validation and the fixed test result

**Answer.** Yes, the output supports an auditable, time-ordered **historical study** of failure-risk ranking under the stated cohort and availability assumptions. The locked test result does not establish that the chosen alert policy is economically better than doing nothing under its declared surrogate. An operational early-warning claim would require verified local vintages, population rules, intervention costs, calibration and external validation.

The seven-feature model table has **1,603,183 bank-quarter rows**: **9,934 positive**, **1,593,249 negative**. The frozen split uses **1984Q1–2012Q4 development**, the four excluded quarters **2013Q1–2013Q4**, and **2014Q1–2025Q2 test**. The test contains **53 distinct represented FDIC failure events**, meeting the assignment's at-least-50 gate; its **178 positive bank-quarter rows are not 178 failures**. Three expanding development folds use four-quarter intervening gaps and training-only preprocessing. Mean fold average precision selected the bounded histogram gradient boosting candidate over the simple logistic baseline using development only; the local Spark MLlib run is a separate fixed demonstration. The OOF ranking and threshold evidence is selection evidence because the same development OOF predictions informed both model and threshold choice.

One full-development refit used **1,329,451** development rows and the locked `SCORE >= 0.03210050445929286` policy. The durable registry records exactly one predictive test-access transition. On **246,083 test rows**, with **178 positive rows** and **53 distinct represented events**, saved-score verification gives **AP 0.172989033**, **ROC-AUC 0.876991628**, **Brier 0.000659085**, and **TP 109, FP 1,483, TN 244,422, FN 69**. **38 of 53** represented events had at least one eligible positive quarter alarm. Precision is **0.068467** and recall **0.612360** at the fixed bank-quarter alarm policy. These row metrics contain repeated observations of banks and events; no independent-event confidence interval was predeclared. Scores are not claimed calibrated.

The development-only COST scale comes from unique verified failure events, but the **0.10 assumed preventable fraction** and **0.01 assumed investigation-cost fraction** are scenario assumptions. Under this fixed **nominal, row-level surrogate**, the test cost is **0.955581967 USD thousands per bank-quarter**, **above** the fixed never-alarm diagnostic of **0.782759273**. This policy is **not economically successful under the declared reference scenario on this test**. AP and ROC-AUC describe ordering across scores; the cost comparison additionally depends on one locked threshold, rare-event prevalence, the false-alarm burden and assumed relative penalties. Good ranking can therefore coexist with a disappointing fixed alarm decision. These are neither literal aggregate FDIC losses nor proven causal benefits of an intervention. The long 46-quarter test horizon may also include distribution shift. No test threshold scan, refit, rescore or policy change followed this result.
